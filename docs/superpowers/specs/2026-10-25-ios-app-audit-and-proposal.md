# iOS App Audit and Browsing Proposal

**Date:** 2026-10-25
**Status:** Proposal. Nothing below is implemented; the owner chooses an approach and answers the decisions first.
**Scope:** the compact phone layout on iOS. The wide web layout is out of scope.

## In plain words

Wist was walked through on an iPhone 17 Pro simulator (Expo Go) with a local catalogue of three houses and four coveted pieces, and a simulated 30 % price drop. The hero loop works: the 60 s poll found the drop, iOS showed the banner, Home showed the wine signal block and Alerts the hero. Six audit lenses (visual design, browsing usability, robustness, iOS platform, accessibility and copy, performance) then read the code and the twenty screenshots and produced 96 findings; duplicates were merged into 75, and every medium or high one was challenged by three independent skeptics (accuracy, product fit against the approved specs, and an adversarial refuter). 39 survived, 5 were refuted as approved decisions or not reproducible, and 31 low ones were kept unverified. The strongest surviving defects are state bugs, not looks: unfollowing a house is undone on the next launch, a notification tap does not open the piece, the catalogue is loaded once so prices go stale after a drop, and a dead backend leaves the app on a spinner with no retry. The look is already coherent; what makes the app weak as a browser is density: no product is visible on Home before scrolling and Discover shows about one and a half pieces per screen. A design canvas shows today's screens next to a product-first proposal.

Design canvas (today vs proposed, iPhone frames): https://claude.ai/artifact/V4Tp4gLzLoFxM28yLzTXTp

### Details and proposed fixes

**F01 · Unfollowing a house is silently undone on every launch**  
*Evidence:* App.tsx:122 calls addFollowedBrands(catalogue.brands.map(b => b.id)) after every loadCatalogue, and again at App.tsx:358 after an import (verified). addFollowedBrands (useWistStore.ts:194-197) is a set union, and followedBrandIds is persisted (partialize, line 382), so a brand removed by toggleFollow (190-193, used by BrowseScreen.tsx:97-103 and ProfileScreen.tsx:147-151) survives only until the next launch, when the union puts it back.  
*Impact:* A user taps FOLLOWING on Sandro to unfollow it, kills the app, and Sandro is back in 'From your houses' and 'The edit'. Follow state is effectively meaningless and a persisted preference is overwritten without feedback.  
*Fix:* Only seed follows on a fresh install: call addFollowedBrands at startup when followedBrandIds still equals the seed default (or a persisted followedBrandsSeeded flag), and in importLiveProduct follow only brand ids absent from the previous catalogue. Both are diffs of data already in memory, no new persisted state. Consequential because it changes which houses get auto-followed on launch; owner confirms the fresh-install rule.

**F02 · Tapping a price-drop notification does not open the piece**  
*Evidence:* notifications.ts:46 attaches data: { alertId, itemId } to every scheduled notification, but nothing consumes it: grep for addNotificationResponseReceivedListener / getLastNotificationResponseAsync / useLastNotificationResponse across App.tsx and src/ returns nothing (verified). App.tsx only installs the handler via import (notifications.ts:7-16) and already has viewAlertItem (App.tsx:299-302) and markAlertRead in the store.  
*Impact:* The banner is the product's core moment. Tapping 'Claudie Pierlot: 30% price drop' foregrounds the app on whatever tab was last open (Feed on cold start, or Profile, Wardrobe, the garment editor); the user then has to find the piece themselves. Every native iOS app deep-links from its own notification.  
*Fix:* In App.tsx call Notifications.useLastNotificationResponse() (built into expo-notifications); in an effect on its change read itemId/alertId from response.notification.request.content.data, keep it in a ref until itemsById has the item, then markAlertRead(alertId) and viewAlertItem(itemId) (or setActiveTab('alerts')). Consequential: new externally visible navigation; the owner picks the landing target (product page vs Alerts hero).

**F03 · Catalogue load is one-shot: a failed or unreachable backend leaves the session empty with developer copy, and Coveted/Alerts claim the user has nothing**  
*Evidence:* App.tsx:116-133 runs loadCatalogue() exactly once (effect deps [addFollowedBrands]); on failure it only console.warn's (126-128). There is no catalogueStatus, no retry, no RefreshControl (WardrobeScreen.tsx:121 is the only onRefresh in the app) and no AppState listener. FeedScreen.tsx:112-116 and BrowseScreen.tsx:117-120 show 'Live catalogue unavailable. Start the catalogue service, then refresh Wist.' whenever the list is empty: during the first second of every launch (liveCatalogue starts empty, App.tsx:58-61, and profileGate does not wait for it), after a real failure, and when the user has unfollowed every house (FeedScreen.tsx:35). SavedScreen.tsx:53 filters persisted starredItemIds through the empty live items, so the header says '0 pieces remembered' and 'Nothing coveted yet' although ids are persisted; AlertsScreen.tsx:38 shows 'The room is quiet' although alerts are persisted. catalogueClient.ts:36-37 defaults the base URL to http://127.0.0.1:4000 (verified), so a physical iPhone without EXPO_PUBLIC_CATALOGUE_URL is permanently in this state.  
*Impact:* A transient network blip at launch makes the user's whole collection appear to vanish, tells them to 'start a service' they do not have and to 'refresh Wist' with no refresh affordance; the only recovery is force-quitting. Every cold start also flashes the error copy on Home/Discover until the last brand resolves.  
*Fix:* Hold catalogueStatus: 'loading' | 'ready' | 'error' in App.tsx next to liveCatalogue and pass it to Feed, Discover, Coveted and Alerts: loading shows 'Loading the index…', error shows 'Couldn't reach the index.' with a primary Button 'Try again' (existing primitive) that calls a lifted reloadCatalogue(), Coveted/Alerts say 'N pieces coveted, reconnect to see them' from the persisted ids, and Feed says 'You follow no houses yet' when brands exist but the edit is empty. Optionally expose reloadCatalogue as onRefresh via RefreshControl on the Feed/Coveted ScrollViews and the Discover FlatList (the WardrobeScreen pattern). Consequential: the retry is new network behaviour and the owner decides manual-only vs automatic; the status/copy part is not.

**F05 · First launch blocks on an un-timed /v1/profile fetch; no catalogue call has a timeout, so a dead backend means a minute of spinner**  
*Evidence:* profileGate.ts:19 returns 'loading' while profileStatus === 'unknown' && !profileLookupComplete; profileLookupComplete is only set in the .finally of synchronizeStyleProfile (App.tsx:171-173), which awaits loadStyleProfile (catalogueClient.ts:511-523). That fetch has no AbortController, unlike saveLocation (catalogueClient.ts:666-667, 10 s) and the wardrobe calls (306/329/377). RN hands timeout 0 to iOS (node_modules/react-native/Libraries/Network/RCTNetworking.mm:335), so the NSURLSession default of ~60 s applies; an unreachable LAN IP can take longer. loadCatalogue (410-437), loadWatches (474-492), loadPriceDropAlerts (494-509), setCatalogueWatch (546-563), saveWatchSizes, importCatalogueWatch and saveStyleProfile have the same gap.  
*Impact:* A new install with the phone on cellular, the Mac asleep, or a stale EXPO_PUBLIC_CATALOGUE_URL shows the wordmark and a spinner for roughly a minute with no message before the intro appears. Later, a covet tap on an unreachable backend leaves the heart busy for the same minute before the 'Could not update Coveted' alert.  
*Fix:* Add one small fetchWithTimeout(ms) helper that wraps the AbortController pattern saveLocation already uses and route the catalogueClient read/write calls through it; the owner picks the value (10 s is the existing precedent) and decides whether the first-run gate should wait for the profile lookup at all (e.g. cap it at a few seconds, then fall through to the intro).
  
*Skeptic's narrower fix:* Keep the approved gate exactly as it is (do not "cap it and fall through to the intro": that re-opens the wizard-races-remote-profile problem the 2026-08-21 spec closed, and with a bounded fetch the lookup rejects, .finally marks it complete, and the spec's "offline fresh install → onboarding" path happens on its own). The only change: a tiny helper in catalogueClient.ts that mirrors saveLocation's AbortController+setTimeout and injects the signal for the seven unbounded calls (simplest form: `withTimeout(fetchImpl, ms): Fetch` so the existing `fetchImpl` default parameter and tests stay untouched). One value, owner-approved before implementing (consequential); 10 s is the existing precedent for small JSON calls. No message changes are needed: catalogueWatchErrorMessage (src/wardrobeMessages.ts:52) already returns the generic "Please try again" for any error, including AbortError.

**F06 · Catalogue prices are loaded once; after a drop the Coveted row and the product page still show the old price**  
*Evidence:* The 60 s poll (App.tsx:229-242) only merges alerts; liveCatalogue is set at startup (118-121) and after an import (357), never otherwise. SavedScreen.tsx:134-135 renders item.currentPrice in wine with drop.oldPrice struck through; with a stale catalogue both numbers are the pre-drop price. ProductDetailScreen.tsx:189-191 shows item.currentPrice/previousPrice from the same stale load. Only FeedScreen.tsx:74-76 and AlertsScreen.tsx:57-58 read the alert's own newPrice, so a Feed signal block can show the new price beside a grid card still showing the launch price.  
*Impact:* The hero moment contradicts itself: the banner and Alerts say 'now € 210', the user taps 'View piece' and the product page says € 300, with € 300 struck through on Coveted. Nothing corrects it until the app is killed and relaunched.  
*Fix:* In synchronizeAlerts, when remoteAlerts[0]?.id differs from the previous poll's newest id, call loadCatalogue() and setLiveCatalogue (same cancelled guard): one extra GET per new drop, nothing on quiet polls. Consequential: adds a network call to the poll; the same reload should run on foreground (F28).

**F07 · Product gallery mounts every media entry at full 2304 px on open**  
*Evidence:* Lines 150-177 render media.map(...) inside a plain horizontal ScrollView (verified line 156), so all entries mount at once. The catalogue stores the gallery at the 2304 px rendition (catalogue_media rows for bobbies-L-M24WO-OPE01: 13 images + 1 video; measured OPERA-...-07-Site.jpg at /2304/ = 1742x2304 px, 510,352 bytes, vs 93,731 bytes for the /1024/ tile). Opening that product requests ~6.5 MB of JPEG plus the 2.85 MB mp4 before the user swipes once. Each image is drawn at mediaWidth x 1.25 = 402x502 pt = 1206x1508 px at 3x; RN iOS downsamples at decode (RCTImageUtils.mm:316-335) so each bitmap is ~7.3 MB, ~95 MB for 13 held while the screen is open, and every one is above RN's 2 MB per-image decoded-cache cap (RCTImageCache.mm:21) so nothing survives a back/forward. The loader runs 4 downloads / 2 decodes concurrently (RCTImageLoader.mm:132-133), so the first visible image competes with 12 siblings.  
*Impact:* On cellular the first product photo appears late while 12 unseen ones download; memory spikes ~95 MB per product open on top of the Feed; re-opening the same product re-decodes everything. Every gallery swipe (setActiveMediaIndex, line 137) re-renders all 14 children.  
*Fix:* Replace the ScrollView+map with a horizontal FlatList (already the codebase's list primitive in Browse/Wardrobe): data={media}, pagingEnabled, keyExtractor={(m, i) => `${m.url}-${i}`}, getItemLayout={(_, i) => ({ length: mediaWidth, offset: mediaWidth * i, index: i })}, initialNumToRender={1}, windowSize={3} so only the active page and its neighbours are mounted; keep onMomentumScrollEnd as is. No timing or network policy changes, so not consequential.

**F08 · Product page is a dead end: tab bar hidden, Back only at the top, no edge-swipe or push transition**  
*Evidence:* App.tsx:470 hides BottomTabBar whenever selectedItemId is set (verified: `!layout.wide && !selectedItemId`). ProductDetailScreen.tsx:143-146 renders the only exit (BackLink) in the top bar of a ScrollView whose media is width×1.25 (line 83); 10-product-bottom shows the page bottom with no exit control. App.tsx:364-389 swaps ProductDetailScreen in by `if (selectedItem)` and 416-433 does the same for outfits/edit/wardrobe; there is no navigation stack, Animated, LayoutAnimation or gesture handling, so screens cut hard and the iOS left-edge swipe does nothing. This is the approved 'no navigation library' decision (spec, Out of scope).  
*Impact:* After reading sizes and 'VIEW ON CLAUDIE PIERLOT' at the bottom, the user must scroll a full screen back up to leave; the habitual edge-swipe fails on the most frequent round-trip in the app; the unread Alerts badge is invisible while on a product.  
*Fix:* Simplest: keep the tab bar mounted on product detail by dropping the `!selectedItemId` condition at App.tsx:470; changeTab (457-461) already clears selectedItemId, so any tab tap exits (this also resolves F59). A genuine push animation and interactive swipe-back cannot be built from ScrollView/Pressable/PanResponder; it needs react-native-screens + @react-navigation/native-stack for a two-route stack (tabs root, product), which contradicts the approved 'no navigation library' decision and must be revisited explicitly if the owner wants the gesture.

**F09 · Intro bottom edge: next page bleeds a wine hairline under panel 03 and the full-bleed pages stop above the home indicator**  
*Evidence:* Pixel crop of 03-intro-watch shows a 1-2px wine line across the full width at the bottom of the stage: setStage({ height: Math.round(height) }) (IntroScreen.tsx:164, verified) sizes every page to a rounded height while pagingEnabled (line 198) snaps by the ScrollView's unrounded frame, so on page 3 the top edge of the wine page 4 shows through. Crops of 01 and 04 show a ~34pt paper strip under the full-bleed cover plate and under the wine hero because App.tsx:281 wraps the intro in SafeAreaView edges={["top","bottom"]} (verified) with a paper container; heroCopy bottom 56 (IntroScreen.tsx:397) and folio bottom 18 (405) are offsets from the stage, not the screen.  
*Impact:* The first thing every new user sees looks cut off: the hero wine page does not own the bottom of the phone, and panel 03 carries a stray red rule that reads as a rendering bug on the most dramatic screen of the first run.  
*Fix:* Store the unrounded height in setStage (or replace pagingEnabled with snapToInterval={stage.height} + decelerationRate="fast"), and give the intro branch edges={["top"]} only, adding useSafeAreaInsets().bottom (react-native-safe-area-context is already the pattern) to heroCopy.bottom, lowerCopy.bottom, coverCopy, folio.bottom and hint.bottom so the plates and wine run under the home indicator. Layout only, no behaviour change.
  
*Skeptic's narrower fix:* One-line fix only: in src/screens/IntroScreen.tsx:164 drop the rounding on the height — `setStage({ width: Math.round(width), height })` — so each page is exactly the ScrollView frame height that pagingEnabled snaps by; everything downstream (range(), safeHeight, the Plate fillHeight/top maths) already tolerates a fractional height. Do not swap pagingEnabled for snapToInterval and do not touch the SafeAreaView edges or add useSafeAreaInsets. If the owner wants the cover plate and wine hero to run under the home indicator, raise that separately as a consequential design question (it reverses the spec's "safe areas respected" decision and needs per-branch edges in App.tsx:281 because Onboarding's footer still needs the bottom inset).

**F10 · Home first viewport is 100% headline type with no product imagery, and the edit is 4 catalogue-order items labelled FOR YOU**  
*Evidence:* 05-home-top: wordmark, four competing headline blocks before any product (titleXL 44 'Pieces worth your attention.', titleL 28 'No meaningful changes yet.', titleL 28 'From your houses', titleL 28 'The edit'); the first product image only appears at the very bottom edge (y≈1750/2000). Spacing stacks: intro.paddingTop 35 + paddingBottom 28 (line 159), quietSignal 22 vertical + marginBottom 38 (line 175), houses.paddingBottom 40 (line 183). FeedScreen.tsx:35 builds the edit as items.filter(followed).slice(0, 4) in catalogue order with no ranking under a 'FOR YOU' kicker (line 109); with all 4 items already coveted, Home shows only what the user already chose.  
*Impact:* For an 'Instagram bones' product browser the home tab shows prose and a 'nothing changed' notice and nothing to browse without scrolling; three 28px serif titles of equal weight give no hierarchy; returning users get the same four pieces every day and a first-time user must guess that Discover is where the products are.  
*Fix:* When there is no alert, fold the quiet block into one kicker line under the dek (e.g. 'WIST IS WATCHING · NO CHANGES YET' in typography.kicker; keep the wine signal block for real drops), cut intro.paddingTop to 20 and houses.paddingBottom to 24, order 'The edit' grid before 'From your houses', raise the edit to 6-8 items and relabel 'FOR YOU' as 'LATEST' until a ranking exists. Pure layout and copy with existing tokens.
  
*Skeptic's narrower fix:* Keep the layout/copy parts (fold the quiet block into one typography.kicker line under the dek, cut intro.paddingTop to 20 and houses.paddingBottom to 24, move the edit grid above "From your houses", raise slice(0, 4) to slice(0, 8)), but do not relabel FOR YOU as LATEST: catalogue-service/src/sqliteRepository.ts returns brand items ORDER BY item.name and catalogueClient.ts:423-436 concatenates per brand, so the first items are alphabetical within the first followed brand and LATEST would be more misleading than FOR YOU. Either drop the sectionNote kicker entirely, or sort the edit client-side by `observedAt` descending (field exists on Item in src/types.ts) before slicing and then label it LATEST. Also note the wide hero (styles.heroWide, intro flex 7 / quiet flex 5) will lose its right column when no alert exists once the quiet block is folded; web is out of scope but keep quietSignalWide rendering on wide so nothing breaks there.

**F11 · Home brand tiles look unfinished: hairline, number and name floating 50pt apart, 'Claudie Pierlot' runs into 'Sandro'**  
*Evidence:* house: { height: 94, justifyContent: "space-between", width: 112 } with houseIndex caption and houseName titleS (lines 184-186), houses.gap: 10. *Skeptic's narrower fix:* If the owner wants to tighten it without changing the idiom: in src/screens/FeedScreen.tsx:183-184 raise houses.gap from 10 to spacing.gutter (or 20) and replace `height: 94, justifyContent: "space-between"` with `gap: 6` so the index sits directly above the name; keep width 112 and numberOfLines={2}. Adding a monogram square to Home (as the finding proposes) is a visible look change that drops the index tying the row to Profile/Coveted and duplicates Profile's monogram StyleSheet; present it to the owner as a design choice to confirm, not as a defect fix.

**F12 · Three different covet affordances across Home, Discover and Product; Home's is a white square flush in the image corner**  
*Evidence:* Home: 42×42 colors.card square at bottom: 0, right: 0 over the image (FeedScreen.tsx:195), wash background when selected. Discover: a text row '♥ COVET / COVETED' under a hairline beneath the price (BrowseScreen.tsx:217-219). *Skeptic's narrower fix:* Leave the three approved forms alone. No new primitive, no new prop. Mark it as a visual decision for the owner (wine heart as the covet motif vs. card heart following the Button's label-colour rule), not a defect.

**F14 · Discover header names the brand twice and sits outside the FlatList, so ~43% of the screen never scrolls away**  
*Evidence:* 07: 'THE WIST INDEX / Discover' (44px), search field, brand tabs with 'Bobbies' selected, hairline, then 'CURATED HOUSE / Bobbies (28px) / 2 pieces selected' + FOLLOWING button (brandHead, lines 90-104, paddingVertical: 19 at line 195). *Impact:* The user reads 'Bobbies' twice within 200px, scrolls past ~130pt of chrome on every brand switch, and then scrolls a letterbox showing ~1.3 pieces under a static block of title, search and tabs; each flick moves so little content the list feels slower than it is.  
*Fix:* Remove the 'CURATED HOUSE / brand name' block and keep a single row under the tabs (`2 pieces` caption left, compact FOLLOW/FOLLOWING Button right, above the hairline), and move the header JSX into ListHeaderComponent on the same FlatList (it already has keyboardShouldPersistTaps="handled"); if the search row should stay reachable, make it the first item with stickyHeaderIndices={[0]}. ## Decisions for the owner

### 1. F01: when should Wist auto-follow catalogue houses?

1. Only on a fresh install: at startup call addFollowedBrands only while followedBrandIds still deep-equals the seed default; after an import, follow only brand ids absent from the previous catalogue (no new persisted key).
2. Add a persisted followedBrandsSeeded: boolean flag and seed once (one new persisted key).
3. Never auto-follow; a house is followed only when the user imports a piece from it or taps FOLLOW.

**Recommendation:** Option 1 — Fixes the silent undo with data already in memory, adds no persisted state, and keeps today's behaviour for a new install. Option 3 would make Home empty for an existing user until they tap FOLLOW.

### 2. F05: what request timeout should the unbounded catalogueClient calls get?

1. 10 000 ms via one withTimeout(fetchImpl, ms) wrapper (the saveLocation precedent), keeping the first-run profile gate waiting as the 2026-08-21 spec requires.
2. 15 000 ms, same wrapper.
3. 5 000 ms for reads, 10 000 ms for writes.

**Recommendation:** Option 1: 10 000 ms, gate unchanged — 10 s is the existing approved value for small JSON calls; with a bounded fetch the lookup rejects, .finally marks it complete and the spec's offline-fresh-install → onboarding path happens on its own. Capping the gate separately would re-open the wizard-races-remote-profile problem.

### 3. F03: how does the user recover from a failed catalogue load?

1. Manual only: catalogueStatus 'loading' | 'ready' | 'error' passed to Feed, Discover, Coveted, Alerts; error state shows a primary Button 'Try again' calling a lifted reloadCatalogue().
2. Option 1 plus pull-to-refresh (RefreshControl, the WardrobeScreen pattern) on Feed, Coveted and Discover.
3. Option 2 plus one automatic retry 5 000 ms after a failure.

**Recommendation:** Option 2 — Both affordances follow existing primitives and only fire on a user gesture; an automatic retry is new network behaviour against a Mac that may simply be asleep. The status/copy half ('Loading the index…', 'N pieces coveted, reconnect to see them') is not consequential and ships regardless.

### 4. F06/F28: when should the catalogue be re-read after launch?

1. Only when the poll sees a new alert id (one extra GET per new drop, nothing on quiet polls).
2. Option 1 plus on every AppState 'active' transition (reloadCatalogue + refreshWatches; no timer juggling, RN already fires the expired poll timer on resume).
3. Option 2 plus every N-th poll (e.g. every 10 minutes) regardless.

**Recommendation:** Option 2 — Covers both ways prices go stale (a drop while open, a day in the background) with the minimum network: a reload per drop and a reload per foreground. Periodic reloads would hit the brand-by-brand endpoint for no visible gain.
